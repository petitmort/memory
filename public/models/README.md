# Sleeping tabby cat

- **Original:** [Sleeping Cat On The Table - 3D Scan](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/sleeping-cat-on-the-table-3d-scan-d9c2ee02107a456eac00356bb43ea4bd)
- **Author:** [Alben Tan](https://sketchfab.com/albentan2012)
- **License:** [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
- **Source archive:** [original glTF, geometry and textures](https://github.com/larissaloza/Stelar-Sanctum-Bad/tree/main/Biblioteca/Modelos/sleeping_cat_on_the_table_-_3d_scan). The original glTF's `asset.extras` identifies the author, title, Sketchfab source and CC BY 4.0 license.

`src/assets/sleeping-tabby.glb` is an adaptation for this project. The surrounding tabletop and hanging original tail were removed. A shorter, curved and tapered tail was reconstructed using the photographed coat. Geometry was conservatively reduced, normals regenerated, and the photographic texture resized to 1024 × 1024 JPEG. The hind legs were folded inward, the head was turned in and lowered toward the paws with a smooth neck blend, and the tail was fitted closely to the flank. Seven stray pale scan triangles beneath the chin were removed. The final curled pose spans about 43 × 32 × 11 cm with its support at Y=0.

The adapted GLB now contains a native skeleton, skin weights and three locally authored clips: `pet-nuzzle`, `pet-stretch` and `pet-tail`. The ten joints cover a fixed root, shoulder, neck, head, two independent ears and a four-joint tail. The head follows continuous pitch, yaw and roll paths; shoulder anticipation, asymmetric ear flicks and delayed tail motion keep the body from moving in lockstep. The ears inherit the head transform instead of sliding against a separately deformed head. Tail weights are isolated by connected topology so the folded hind leg beneath the tail stays still. Smooth weight transitions preserve the photographed UVs. The paws, support plane, rear body and tail attachment remain grounded.

`src/cat-choreography.ts` defines a 4.4-second response with passing-pose velocity rather than a stop at every key. The existing meow remains at 2.760–3.312 seconds, followed by a longer quiet settling motion. `src/cat-articulation.ts` defines local joint transforms. `scripts/rig-sleeping-cat.mjs` bakes those curves at 60 Hz into standard glTF translation and quaternion tracks, using the existing Node and Three.js dependencies. `src/cat-rig.ts` plays those embedded clips through `AnimationMixer`; it no longer builds morph targets at load time. Reduced-motion playback blends the same clips toward the bind pose with weight 0.16. On completion or cancellation, joint transforms return exactly to the original sleeping pose. `src/sleeping-cat.ts` retains the small grounded breathing motion, per-playback audio clock, muted scene-time clock, input cooldown and background cancellation.

The source mesh, indices, normals, photographed UVs, material and embedded texture are preserved during rigging. The asset has 11,608 vertices, 20,207 triangles, one mesh/material and one 1024 × 1024 JPEG. The GLB is 1,129,876 bytes (368,904 bytes more than the previous static asset). Skin indices/weights use 232,160 bytes, inverse bind matrices 640 bytes, and the three clips plus their shared time array 125,080 bytes. Each clip has 265 samples and ten tracks. The former 1,114,368-byte runtime morph position/normal buffers are removed; a small bone-matrix texture replaces the morph-target GPU path. These figures describe animation data, not whole-page memory. Animated bounds include the head and tail for frustum culling and picking. Skinning resources are prepared before the existing room shader/GPU warmup; playing a gesture reuses them. Vite emits a content-hashed GLB filename. The adapted GLB includes attribution and modification notes in `asset.extras`. Visible attribution for the scan and the separate purr-and-meow recording is available from the room's “猫咪素材鸣谢” link to [the credits page](../credits.html); sound provenance is documented in [the audio notes](../audio/README.md).

## Public animation research

We reviewed the following openly licensed candidates:

- [Sphynx Cat – Rigged & Animated](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/sphynx-cat-rigged-animated-10-anims-0b86bde7a8b64fd7a459035b339a790f), Animal Animator, CC BY 4.0. The [author's anatomical rig article](https://animalanimator.com/the-ultimate-sphinx-cat-reference-for-animal-animators/) informed the use of shoulder/neck overlap, independent ears and a segmented tail. Its standing hairless body and locomotion clips do not match the current curled tabby scan.
- [Cat Sleep on Sofa](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/cat-sleep-on-sofa-c0d37fb8a25a49639dd8317ad68701de), hugannh3, CC BY 4.0. This stylized game scene includes one animation; a reusable independent cat skeleton was not verified.
- [Ultimate Animated Animal Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/ultimateanimatedanimals.html), Quaternius, CC0. A stylized alternative; a matching curled cat and petting clip were not verified.

No geometry, animation file, photograph or video from these candidates was imported. The current rig and clips are authored for this project, and the existing scan remains the sole source of the cat's photographed appearance. The scan still has closed eyes and a fixed mouth; it does not contain facial animation, motion capture, hair simulation or a complete walking rig. Runtime rendering and numerical skin tests do not establish real-device frame rates.
